Buying from Japanese auctions: grades, JEVIC and what they mean

Japan is the world's most organised used-vehicle export market. Cars reach export through manufacturer auctions, dealer auctions and trade stock, and each comes with paperwork that you should read before paying.

Auction grades

A grade is a condition score, not a warranty. A 4.5 car is usually clean with minor marks; a 3.5 car may have panel repairs or visible wear. Grades do not tell you whether the mileage is genuine on their own, and they are not a substitute for photographs of the actual unit.

JEVIC and independent inspection

JEVIC is a Japanese export inspection certificate. It gives you a documented condition record at the port, which matters if something is disputed later. We ask for the JEVIC sheet plus a walk-round video before the purchase stage is authorised.

Mileage and service history

Japanese auction sheets show recorded mileage and often the auction house's own inspection notes. Cross-check the mileage against the service stickers and the auction sheet: a car with unusually low mileage and no history is a reason to pause, not a bargain to grab.

What we check before your money moves

Supplier licence, the chassis number on the invoice against the inspection report, the auction sheet, photographs and video, and the shipping schedule from Yokohama. Only then does the quotation become a purchase.
