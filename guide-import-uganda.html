Importing a vehicle into Uganda: duty, excise and VAT explained

Uganda charges three main taxes on imported vehicles: import duty, excise duty and value added tax. They are calculated in a chain, and that chain is why two vehicles with the same price can land at very different totals.

The CIF value comes first

Duty is charged on the CIF value: the cost of the vehicle, plus insurance, plus freight. A cheap vehicle with expensive freight can therefore carry more tax than expected. Our calculator builds the CIF value from the supplier price, marine insurance and the freight quote for the route.

Duty, then excise, then VAT

Import duty applies to the CIF value. Excise applies to the same value but at a rate that rises with engine size, which is why a 2.7 litre Prado attracts more excise than a 1.0 litre Vitz. VAT is then charged on the CIF value plus duty plus excise. Each line is shown separately in the calculator so you can see exactly what the engine size is costing you.

Age and drive side

Uganda requires right-hand drive vehicles, and older vehicles face restrictions. Before a deposit is taken, we check the year of manufacture against the rule in force and tell you if the unit is at risk. If eligibility is uncertain, we pause rather than take payment.

Why estimates need confirmation

URA applies its own valuation in some cases, and rates change. The figures from our calculator are estimates until a licensed clearing agent confirms them for your specific chassis number. That confirmation is what a final quotation is based on.

This guide is general information, not customs or legal advice. Rules change and are applied case by case.
