Container shipping, RoRo and what your bill of lading tells you

Most vehicles moving to East Africa travel either in a container or on a RoRo vessel. Each has trade-offs, and your bill of lading is the document that actually proves the shipment.

Container versus RoRo

A container protects the vehicle, allows spare parts to travel with it, and can be shared between two or three vehicles. RoRo is usually cheaper per unit for a single running vehicle but exposes the bodywork and cannot carry loose parts.

What the bill of lading proves

The BL is the contract between shipper and carrier. It records the vessel, the container or unit, the ports and the consignee. It is not proof of condition and it is not proof of payment, which is why we keep the inspection report separately on your order file.

Insurance from the yard, not the port

Marine insurance should start when the vehicle leaves the supplier's yard, not when it reaches the port. Inland damage between the two is common and uninsured if cover starts too late.

When a vessel is delayed

Delays happen. We post tracking events manually when a carrier has no API, and we tell you the new ETA rather than leaving a stale date on your order. Live vessel tracking is only shown when a carrier feed is actually connected and confirmed.

This guide is general information, not customs or legal advice. Rules change and are applied case by case.
